# Evidence Rules

## Source Priority

1. current official application page and announcement;
2. official company, laboratory, product, patent, standard, conference, journal, and university pages;
3. peer-reviewed paper and attributable author affiliation;
4. ORCID, professional homepage, Google Scholar, or public professional profile;
5. reputable technical media;
6. salary platform, offer community, forum, or social post.

Lower-priority sources can identify leads but cannot silently override current official evidence.

## Confidence Labels

- `confirmed`: directly stated by an official or primary source;
- `corroborated`: supported by at least two independent credible sources;
- `reasonable inference`: consistent with evidence but not explicitly stated;
- `unconfirmed`: plausible lead that must not drive a final claim.

Attach confidence to team mapping, current employment, hiring-manager identity, location, level, and compensation.

## Identity and Team Mapping

Before attributing work to a person, match name, employer, field, coauthors, location, and timeline. Distinguish current and historical employment.

Map a team in decreasing certainty:

1. official JD names it;
2. official team page connects role and people;
3. repeated recent outputs connect people, employer, and topic;
4. public professional profiles describe the unit;
5. infer only the broad organization and label uncertainty.

Do not construct an unsupported organization chart or collect irrelevant personal information.

## Research Evidence

Prefer repeated patterns over isolated outputs:

- recent three-year trajectory when available;
- recurring venues and standards bodies;
- repeated technical objects, methods, data, and platforms;
- deployment, standardization, or productization evidence;
- authorship and contribution when supportable.

A venue or keyword alone does not prove a research direction.

## Compensation Evidence

Separate:

- official program guarantee;
- attributable offer sample;
- self-reported platform sample;
- recruiter estimate;
- model inference.

Normalize year, city, role family, degree, salary months, cash, bonus, stock, sign-on, housing, and government subsidy. Use ranges when samples are sparse and prefer a median or central cluster over an arithmetic average.
