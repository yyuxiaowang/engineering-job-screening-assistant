# Engineering PhD Candidate Fingerprint

## Progressive Loading

For intake and scope refinement, build a provisional fingerprint from a small set of high-signal materials: the controlling resume/profile, publication list, and concise research or project summaries. Read only enough to recommend recruitment projects and official filters. Do not read every full paper, thesis chapter, presentation, or historical application before the official recruitment scope is confirmed. Load deeper evidence only when it can resolve an L2-L4 decision.

If an authorized folder contains several plausible resume or profile versions, use only facts that are consistent across them for scope refinement. Ask which version controls only when a material conflict could change eligibility, project selection, or official filter recommendations; never choose solely by filename or timestamp.

## Hierarchical Dimensions

Describe both candidate and role using the same dimensions:

1. engineering family;
2. subfields;
3. research object or engineered system;
4. technical layer: material, device, circuit, physical/link, protocol/network, control, system, algorithm, application;
5. problem class: design, transmission, scheduling, diagnosis, optimization, control, prediction, inference, verification, manufacturing;
6. methods and mathematical foundations;
7. software, hardware, instruments, protocols, and standards;
8. experimental or deployment environment;
9. artifacts: papers, patents, standards, prototypes, products, deployments;
10. recency, depth, and candidate contribution.

## Evidence Tiers

- `core`: repeated PhD work or recent primary-author output;
- `direct engineering`: substantial prototype, experiment, standard, product, or implementation evidence;
- `credible adjacent`: another degree, meaningful project, internship, or recurring method with a defensible bridge;
- `foundational exposure`: coursework or a skills-list claim;
- `unsupported`: no supplied evidence;
- `unwanted`: work the candidate explicitly excludes.

Do not describe foundational exposure as direct expertise.

## Transfer-Bridge Test

Before rejecting an adjacent engineering subfield, test whether at least one material bridge exists:

- shared engineered system or interface;
- shared physical or mathematical model;
- shared protocol, standard, or architecture;
- shared technical layer and problem class;
- shared experimental platform or instrumentation;
- official JD explicitly seeks the transferable method;
- candidate has a publication, prototype, project, or substantial prior-stage project supporting the transition.

A shared generic tool alone is weak evidence.

## Standard-Dependent Decisions

### Strict Specialist

Require core or direct engineering evidence in the same object/layer, or a documented transfer bridge explicitly accepted by the JD. Adjacent evidence is a fallback, not automatically a recommendation.

### Broad Engineering

Keep candidates within the accepted engineering family through early stages when the specialization is ambiguous. Use the full JD to decide whether the daily work remains credible.

### Pooled Allocation

Evaluate the organization and disclosed subdirections, not only the umbrella title. Record uncertainty about final assignment.

## False-Friend Check

Identical words may refer to different work:

- `network` may mean communication, computer, energy, transportation, or neural network;
- `model` may mean physical, statistical, machine-learning, or product data model;
- `algorithm` may mean signal processing, control, optimization, application ranking, or infrastructure;
- `system` may refer to hardware, protocol architecture, software platform, or operations.

Confirm object, layer, data, deliverable, and research community before declaring a match.
